### List C: Anywhere Code or Docs Call a Metric Misleading, Biased, or Conditionally Invalid

Direct documentation and code findings where specific metrics are critiqued, refuted, or identified as flawed:

1. **Roy-Vetterli Effective Rank (`style_metrics._effective_rank`) on Concatenated Singular Values**
   - *Sources*: `LATENT-AUG.md` §5, `probes/probe_style_dimensionality.py:65-68, 213-216`, `research-briefs/results/ASSESSMENT-2026-08-17.md` (Ledger A\'\'9/E13), `PROJECT-STATE.md:234`.
   - *Why Misleading/Invalid*: The repo's `_effective_rank` applies the Roy-Vetterli spectral entropy formula to the singular values of concatenated rollout frames. This produces a severe illusion of full dimensionality (reporting 62.8 / 64, appearing near-isotropic) when the true Participation Ratio of covariance eigenvalues is only ~12–15 / 64. The singular value metric conflates frame-stack/temporal shifts with true style diversity across environments and is documented as *actively misleading*.

2. **KAGE Task-Defined Binary Success Rate (`success_once` / `eval_eval_success`)**
   - *Sources*: `CLAIMS-LEDGER.md` (E1, E2, E3, E5, E24, E26), `PROBLEMS.md` (P-01, P-24, P-25), `reports/2026-08-17-audit-and-factorial/REPORT.md:90, 266`, `UNDERSTANDING.md`.
   - *Why Misleading/Invalid*: KAGE binary success is saturated and *blind-solvable*: an open-loop script that jumps right on every step achieves a success rate of 1.000 on flat/easy/mid/hard configs and 0.938 on `config_5_train` without processing visual inputs. Therefore, success rate alone cannot test visual generalization. Furthermore, reward optimum does not equal success optimum: constant jumping maximizes success (0.938) but destroys return ($-4981.5$) due to jump penalties, whereas sighted policies achieve $-1255.7$ return at $0.594$ success.

3. **Local / Per-Step Policy Consistency KL Divergence (`policy_consistency_kl` / `local_kl`)**
   - *Sources*: `CLAIMS-LEDGER.md` (E21, E30), `reports/phase3-procgen-transfer/REPORT.md:78`, `research-briefs/results/ASSESSMENT-2026-08-17.md:30-32`, `audit/SYNTHESIS.md:99`.
   - *Why Misleading/Invalid*: Local per-step KL divergence is heavy-tailed (mean 0.442 vs median 0.004) and fails to predict global trajectory behavior or episodic success. A policy with high per-step consistency (local KL 0.0885, top-1 agreement 0.9870) can completely collapse over a 500-step episode (success collapsing from 1.000 to 0.281, return $-1346.1$). In addition, weak or high-entropy policies artificially achieve tiny KL divergence everywhere simply because their action probabilities are flat (peakedness confound).

4. **Best-of-N Peak Held-Out Success / Return (`--best-checkpoint-metric`) Without Confidence Intervals**
   - *Sources*: `CLAIMS-LEDGER.md` (E29, E31, E33, F-21), `PROJECT-STATE.md:104`, `reports/2026-08-17-audit-and-factorial/REPORT.md:266`.
   - *Why Misleading/Invalid*: Selecting the maximum evaluated score across training updates is a heavily biased estimator due to best-of-N selection bias / cherry-picking. A training run whose held-out success fluctuates wildly (e.g. 0.000 $\to$ 0.828 $\to$ 0.000 $\to$ 0.531) is reported as 0.828, while its final policy is 0.117. When Wilson 95% confidence intervals are computed, the claimed separation between latent augmentation (0.844) and procedural/baseline (0.977) overlaps.

5. **Linear Probe $R^2$ Without Held-Out Split**
   - *Sources*: `jax-ood-gen/rl_algorithms/disentaglement/code_ppo/style_metrics.py:24-27`, `research/harness.py:166-169`.
   - *Why Misleading/Invalid*: In-sample linear probe $R^2$ suffers from severe in-sample inflation / overfitting: pure Gaussian noise can achieve $R^2 \approx 1.0$ when feature dimension is large relative to sample count ($D \approx N$). A strict held-out 2-fold cross-validation split with ridge regularization is required to ensure that uninformative latents yield $R^2 \le 0$.

6. **Far-Field Terrain Probe $R^2$ Without Autocorrelation Baseline**
   - *Sources*: `research/probes/q49_terrain.py:8-12, 75-78`.
   - *Why Misleading/Invalid*: Reporting that task latent $z_{\mathrm{task}}$ predicts future terrain $Y_{t+k}$ with $R^2 = 0.86$ is misleading because natural terrain is smooth and autocorrelated ($Y_t$ alone predicts $Y_{t+k}$ with $R^2 = 0.85$). The net information gain of the representation is only $R^2 - R^2_{\mathrm{auto}} \approx 0.01$. Terrain probes must always report gain over autocorrelation.

7. **Global Pixel MSE / L1 Reconstruction Loss on Imbalanced Visual Scenes**
   - *Sources*: `KAGE-RECON-INVESTIGATION.md`, `probes/probe_what_the_decoder_drops.py:9-19`, `audit/D_report_and_audits.md`.
   - *Why Misleading/Invalid*: Global pixel reconstruction losses are overwhelmingly dominated by background pixels (95%+ of the image). The playable agent sprite occupies $<1.4\%$ (often $\sim 0.2\%$) of pixels and platforms $\sim 3\%$. A decoder can achieve near-zero global MSE / L1 loss while completely erasing the player character and platforms. Semantic region-masked correlation and normalized error are necessary.

8. **Invariance Loss on Degenerate / Single-Color Backgrounds**
   - *Sources*: `research/probes/q52_augmentation_strength.py:6-12`, `CLAIMS-LEDGER.md` (D2, D4).
   - *Why Misleading/Invalid*: In environments with solid black backgrounds (`config_8_train`), style diversity collapses to zero ($z_{\mathrm{style}}$ $\mu$ std = 0.0016), causing augmented frames to be identical to clean frames. The policy invariance loss becomes $\sim 0$ by construction, giving a false appearance of policy robustness that collapses when tested on colored backgrounds.

9. **Unregularized Calibration Ratios with Vanishing Denominators**
   - *Sources*: `PROJECT-STATE.md:71, 264`, `reports/phase3-procgen-transfer/REPORT.md:24-30`, `research-briefs/results/2026-08-16_latent-style-augmentation-survey.md:107-121`.
   - *Why Misleading/Invalid*: Computing calibration ratios $\frac{\mathrm{KL}_{\mathrm{aug}}}{\mathrm{KL}_{\mathrm{envstyle}}}$ where the denominator $\mathrm{KL}_{\mathrm{envstyle}}$ is degenerate / near-zero (e.g. 0.0001) causes explosive variance and undefined division. Ratios require regularized floors ($\epsilon$) and logarithmic transformation with bootstrap confidence intervals.

10. **Generalization Gap on Asymmetric Baselines (Fine-Tuning vs Un-Fine-Tuned Control)**
    - *Sources*: `CLAIMS-LEDGER.md` (E26, E29, E32), `reports/2026-08-17-audit-and-factorial/REPORT.md`.
    - *Why Misleading/Invalid*: Measuring fine-tuned augmented models against the un-fine-tuned base checkpoint attributes the entire performance gain ($0.19 \to 0.83$) to augmentation. However, a plain fine-tuning control without augmentation on the same training steps achieves $0.898$–$0.938$. The comparison confounded additional gradient steps with augmentation benefit.
