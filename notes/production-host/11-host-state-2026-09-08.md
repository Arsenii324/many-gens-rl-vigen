# Which baselines fit *surely*, and why the answer is not "the smallest"

The question is not peak size. It is **whether the peak was measured at the configuration
production will run.** VRAM scales with environment parallelism, not with the frame budget — the
replay buffer lives in host RAM, not on the card.

| family | observed peak | parallelism measured → production | safe to read as representative? |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `ppg` | 6.98 GiB | `num_envs` 1 → 1 | **yes** |
| `rlvigen` | 4.44 GiB | single env → single env | **yes** |
| `idaac` | 1.79 GiB | `num_processes` 1 → 1 | **yes** |
| `alda` | 1.39 GiB | single env → single env | **yes** |
| `dmc_gb` | 1.36 GiB | single env → single env | **yes** |
| `ibac_sni` | 0.88 GiB | `procs` **1 → 16** | **no** — 16× the workers |
| `ctrl` | 20.78 GiB | `num_envs` **16 → 64** | **no** — 4× the envs, from an already-large base |

**Five of seven were measured at their production parallelism.** `rlvigen`, `dmc_gb`, `alda`,
`idaac` and `ppg` change only their frame budget between the measured cell and the production one,
and frames do not drive VRAM. Those are the ones that fit surely.

The two that do not are the two already flagged elsewhere for the same reason:

- **`ctrl`** — 20.78 GiB at 16 envs, production is 64. Its host-RAM figure is likewise an
  unverified 4× extrapolation. Both of its production resource figures are unmeasured, in the same
  direction. It must be measured alone, capped, and watched.
- **`ibac_sni`** — 0.88 GiB at `procs=1`, production is `procs=16`. The smallest observed peak in
  the fleet is also one of the two least trustworthy, which is exactly the trap in ranking by size.

**Order of first production runs, on this evidence:** `dmc_gb`, `alda`, `idaac` (all well under
2 GiB at matched parallelism), then `rlvigen`, then `ppg`. `ctrl` and `ibac_sni` only after their
own measurement.

# The card is the GROUP's, not ours alone

The assignment grants **our group** use of `cds2` `V100-1`. It is not a personal reservation and it
does not guarantee exclusivity: a colleague's job on that card is legitimate and expected, not an
intrusion. Earlier notes in this directory framed the assigned card as though it were ours alone —
that framing was wrong and this supersedes it.

Two consequences:

- **Never assume the card will stay free.** A groupmate may start on it at any time, with every
  right to. This makes the upper-bound rule stricter here, not looser: our run must leave room, and
  we must not be the reason someone else's fails.
- **Coordination beats measurement.** If the card is occupied, the useful question is *whose and
  for how long* — asked of the owner or the group, **not** answered by enumerating containers.

## Attributing a load, when it is genuinely necessary

`nvidia-smi` reports **current** state only; a load that has ended cannot be attributed after the
fact, and no amount of looking will recover it.

If a load is live and attribution actually matters, the order is:

1. `nvidia-smi --query-compute-apps=pid,used_memory --format=csv` — PID and VRAM, **without**
   enumerating containers, images or paths.
2. If that PID is **ours**, identify it entirely within our own scope.
3. If it is **not ours**, stop and ask the owner or the group. Do **not** run `docker ps`,
   `docker inspect`, or read another user's paths to put a name to it. That is the line
   `05-privacy-and-non-alarm.md` draws, and being right about whose job it is does not move it.

# Monitoring the card: occasionally, never as a standing job

**Do not leave a polling process running on the host.** A recurring job that repeatedly queries the
GPU is the shape of a resource-scraping script and can trip security tooling, which costs the
account and the project with it.

Check **occasionally and by hand**, with the aggregate query that does not enumerate processes:

```bash
ssh -o BatchMode=yes varaksin_as@100.98.2.11 \
  'nvidia-smi --query-gpu=index,memory.used,memory.free,utilization.gpu --format=csv,noheader'
```

Reasonable moments: before starting a run; once after it has settled into steady state; if
something looks wrong. **Not on a timer, and not in a loop.**

The DataSphere job monitors this session runs are a different thing entirely — they poll the
DataSphere API from *this* laptop, not the host, and touch `cds2` not at all.

## What this changes about "clone the repo instead"

Cloning is not the shortcut it looks like, on three counts:

- It writes to the **same 99% root** — the disk problem is unchanged.
- The repository is **private**, so a clone needs credentials on the host. Putting them there means
  configuring the host, which `02-absolute-prohibitions.md` forbids outright.
- It **bypasses the payload contract.** `contract.py verify-payload` and `verify-evaluator-binding`
  are what tie a returned record to a specific tree; a working copy assembled by `git clone` has no
  such binding, and records produced from it could not be attested. The payload path is not
  bureaucracy — it is the mechanism that makes results traceable.

The payload is also *smaller*: ~250 KB for a family's code, against a repository with a 59 MB
`.git`.
